How a ridge point gets its elevation

Plan view — two drain groups, one roof

Drains at (2.0, 2.0) m and (8.0, 0.0) m — deliberately off-centre. The ridge lands wherever the roof-path distances to each drain tie, not at the geometric middle.

Drain Ridge point Basin edge
1Group drainsDrains within the group radius (1000 mm) become one drain; each remaining group is a separate outlet.
2Path-distance basinsEvery roof point joins the basin of whichever group is closer along the roof surface — around openings, along arcs.
3Find the ridgePoints sitting on a basin boundary are ridge points — this is the Voronoi seam, so it's rarely centred.
4Lift to the far drainRidge height = slope × path to the farthest surrounding basin, so every side gets at least the given slope.
5Move the watershedPoints behind a ridge re-flow to the far drain at exactly the given slope — nothing ponds.
In practice — six randomly placed drains

Same 18 × 11 m roof, drains dropped without a pattern

Two drains land close together and merge into one outlet (dashed circle = the 1.0 m group radius); the other four stay separate. Five outlets in total, each pulling its own basin — every seam between two basins is a run of ridge points, raised just enough to still hit 1.5% towards the farther side.
